\title[Vertical supports and the hypercube tree formula]{Spanning trees of
$G\times K_2$ by vertical support, partial doubles,\\ and the bijective
program for the hypercube tree formula}
\author{Tyler Aden}
\date{Draft of August 19, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
For a connected graph $G$, we count the spanning trees of the prism
$G\times K_2$ by their \emph{vertical support} --- the set $S$ of vertices
of $G$ whose vertical edge is used: the count is
$\tfrac12\,c(G)\,2^{|S|}F_G(S)$, where $c(G)$ is the number of spanning
trees and $F_G(S)$ the number of $S$-rooted spanning forests of $G$.
Iterating this identity over the $n$ directions of the hypercube
$Q_n = Q_{n-1}\times K_2$ telescopes, through Pascal's rule, to Stanley's
product formula $c(Q_n)=2^{2^n-n-1}\prod_k k^{\binom nk}$, giving an
elementary inductive derivation of that formula and, more importantly,
reducing the problem of a fully bijective proof --- open since
\cite[Example~5.6.10]{ec2}, and still open in bijective form after
Bernardi's combinatorial proofs \cite{bernardi} --- to a single per-level
bijective step. We then prove the \emph{class-level law}: refining by the
multiset $U$ of level-projected horizontal edges, the trees in each class
$(S,U)$ number exactly $d(S,U)\,2^{|S|-1}$, where $d(S,U)$ counts the
splittings of $U$ into a spanning tree and an $S$-rooted forest of $G$ ---
and each splitting occurs among the trees of its class as an explicit
\emph{polarized} representative. This proves every counting statement in
the program down to its finest stratum; in the rooted setting we prove an all-spins-zero
normal form theorem and its colored-forest generalization, which, combined
with the explicit spin-flip involutions furnished by Bernardi's inductive
proof of spin independence, assemble into an explicit algorithmic bijection
between rooted spanning trees of $Q_n$ and tuples of colors in
$\prod_k [2k]^{\binom nk}$ --- a Pr\"ufer code for the hypercube, and with
it a bijective proof of Stanley's formula. The construction is implemented
and machine-verified (exhaustively for $n \le 3$; sampled at $n = 4$); its
one recursive ingredient, the spin normalization, remains to be made
closed-form. We also
record an exactly verified spin-uniformity phenomenon (Bernardi's
independence holds exactly for rooted-anywhere trees and provably fails
for root-fixed ones) that constrains the shape of any such map.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background}

Stanley's \emph{Enumerative Combinatorics} vol.~2 derives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stanley}
  c(Q_n) \;=\; 2^{2^n-n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n} k^{\binom nk}
\end{equation}
from the Laplacian eigenvalues of $Q_n$ and remarks (1st ed., p.~62,
closing Example~5.6.10) that ``a direct combinatorial proof of this formula
is not known.'' Bernardi \cite{bernardi} gave two combinatorial proofs ---
one via an independence property of edge-direction ``spins'' in uniformly
random rooted forests, one via killing involutions --- and the second
edition of \cite{ec2} (2024, Notes to Ch.~5) updates the remark: a
\emph{bijective} proof is not known. This paper organizes a route to one.

Throughout, $G$ is a connected graph on $M$ vertices (for
\eqref{eq:stanley}, $G = Q_{n-1}$ and $M = 2^{n-1}$), and
$G\times K_2$ is the prism over $G$: two copies $G\times\{0\}$,
$G\times\{1\}$ (\emph{horizontal} edges) plus a \emph{vertical} edge
$\{(u,0),(u,1)\}$ for each $u\in V(G)$. For a spanning tree $T$ of
$G\times K_2$, let $S(T)\subseteq V(G)$ be its vertical support. For
$S \subseteq V(G)$, let $F_G(S)$ denote the number of spanning forests of
$G$ with exactly $|S|$ components, each containing one vertex of $S$; by
the all-minors Matrix-Tree theorem $F_G(S) = \det L_G[\bar S]$, the minor
of the Laplacian with the rows and columns of $S$ removed.

\section{The support-product theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:support}
For every nonempty $S\subseteq V(G)$, the number of spanning trees $T$ of
$G\times K_2$ with $S(T)=S$ is
\[
  N_G(S) \;=\; \tfrac12\, c(G)\; 2^{|S|}\, F_G(S).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Weight the vertical edge at $u$ by an indeterminate $z_u$ and every
horizontal edge by $1$, and let $t(z)=\sum_T\prod_{u\in S(T)}z_u$ be the
weighted spanning-tree sum. With vertices ordered level by level, the
weighted Laplacian is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix} L_G+Z & -Z\\ -Z & L_G+Z \end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
$Z=\operatorname{diag}(z_u)$, which the orthogonal level-swap basis
$\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix} I & I\\ I & -I
\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ block-diagonalizes as $L_G\oplus(L_G+2Z)$. For a
weighted graph on $N$ vertices the product of the nonzero Laplacian
eigenvalues is $N$ times the tree sum, and the kernel lies in the $L_G$
block; with $N = 2M$,
\[
  t(z) \;=\; \frac{1}{2M}\,\Bigl(\;\prod_{\lambda\ne0}\lambda(L_G)\Bigr)
  \det(L_G+2Z)
  \;=\; \frac{c(G)}{2}\,\det(L_G+2Z).
\]
Expanding by principal minors in the diagonal matrix $2Z$ and applying the
all-minors Matrix-Tree theorem,
$\det(L_G+2Z)=\sum_{R}2^{|R|}F_G(R)\prod_{u\in R}z_u$. Both sides are
multilinear in the $z_u$; compare coefficients of $\prod_{u\in S}z_u$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The identity is elementary and product-graph Matrix-Tree computations of
this kind appear in Martin--Reiner \cite{mr} and Bernardi
\cite[\S4]{bernardi}; we make no novelty claim for
Theorem~\ref{thm:support} itself. Its role here is architectural: it is the
exact statement whose bijective realization would prove
\eqref{eq:stanley} bijectively (Corollary~\ref{cor:telescope} and
Problem~\ref{prob:main}). We first found it as an experimental identity:
it was confirmed class-by-class for $Q_2,Q_3,Q_4$ ($3+15+255$ supports; the
$Q_4$ case over all $679\,477\,248$ tree--root pairs) before the proof was
written down.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:telescope}
Iterating Theorem~\ref{thm:support} yields \eqref{eq:stanley}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Summing the theorem over all $S$ (the $S=\varnothing$ term vanishes) and
using $\sum_R z^{|R|}F_G(R) = \det(zI+L_G)$ at $z=2$:
\[
  c(G\times K_2) \;=\; \tfrac12\,c(G)\,\det(2I+L_G).
\]
For $G=Q_{n-1}$, whose Laplacian eigenvalues are $2k$ with multiplicity
$\binom{n-1}k$, $\det(2I+L)=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+2)^{\binom{n-1}k}$.
Writing $R_n=2^n c(Q_n)$, the recursion reads
$R_n = R_{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^n(2j)^{\binom{n-1}{j-1}}$, and Pascal's rule
gives $R_n=\prod_{j=1}^n(2j)^{\binom nj}$ by induction, which is
\eqref{eq:stanley}.
\end{proof}

Of course, evaluating $\det(2I+L_{Q_{n-1}})$ spectrally re-uses linear
algebra; combinatorially, $\det(2I+L_G) = \sum_S 2^{|S|}F_G(S)$ counts
\emph{pairs} (an $S$-rooted spanning forest, a $\{0,1\}$-coloring of its
roots), so the recursion has the set-level form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:setform}
\begin{gathered}
  \{\text{spanning trees of } G\times K_2\}\ \times\ \{0,1\}\\
  \longleftrightarrow\quad
  \{\text{spanning trees of }G\}\ \times\
  \{\text{root-$2$-colored rooted spanning forests of } G\},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
and a bijective proof of \eqref{eq:stanley} needs exactly a constructive
form of \eqref{eq:setform}, level by level.

\section{Bijective fragments}

\subsection{The full-support slice}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:full}
A spanning tree of $G\times K_2$ containing every vertical edge is the same
data as a spanning tree of $G$ together with a level in $\{0,1\}$ for each
of its $M-1$ edges: contract the vertical edges to project, and lift each
base tree edge to its recorded level to invert. Hence
$N_G(V(G)) = c(G)\,2^{M-1}$, the $S=V(G)$ case of
Theorem~\ref{thm:support}.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Partial doubles}

\begin{definition}
For $S\subseteq V(G)$, the \emph{partial double} $G^{(S)}$ is the graph
obtained from $G$ by splitting each $u\notin S$ into $u_0,u_1$: an edge
$(u,v)$ of $G$ becomes two parallel edges when $u,v\in S$; the two edges
$(u,v_0),(u,v_1)$ when $u\in S$, $v\notin S$; and $(u_0,v_0),(u_1,v_1)$
when $u,v\notin S$.
\end{definition}

Contracting the vertical edges of a tree with support $S$ is a bijection
onto the spanning trees of $G^{(S)}$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:support} is
equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial}
  c\bigl(G^{(S)}\bigr) \;=\; \tfrac12\,c(G)\,2^{|S|}\,F_G(S).
\end{equation}
For $S=V(G)$ this is Proposition~\ref{prop:full} (all edges doubled); for
$S=\varnothing$ both sides vanish on connectivity grounds. Splitting one
vertex at a time interpolates between $G^{(V)}$ and $G^{(S)}$ and is a
natural inductive route to a bijection, though the ratio
$F_G(S)/F_G(S\cup u)$ is not local, so the induction must carry more
structure than a single count.

\subsection{What the spins do and do not give}

Bernardi's independence theorem \cite[Thm.~1]{bernardi} concerns uniformly
random rooted forests conditioned on their projection; transported to
spanning trees it says the \emph{spins} (for each $u\in S$, the level of
the endpoint of the vertical edge at $u$ nearer the root) should be free
bits. We verified computationally: for the ensemble of (tree, root) pairs
of $Q_3$, conditioned on the direction and the support $S$, the joint
distribution of the spins is \emph{exactly} uniform on $\{0,1\}^S$ (all
$45$ conditioned classes, zero discrepancy), and remains consistent with
uniform on samples of $2\times10^6$ ($Q_4$) and $10^6$ ($Q_5$) uniform
trees. For trees with a \emph{fixed} root the uniformity provably fails
(e.g.\ $\chi^2=330.7$ on $65$ dof already for $Q_3$). Thus the factor
$2^{|S|}$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:support} is realized by free bits only in the
rooted-anywhere ensemble; any bijection must fix the root only after the
bits are extracted. This matches the shape of \eqref{eq:setform}, which is
stated for a $2$-fold cover of the tree set rather than for rooted trees.

\section{The class-level law}\label{sec:fine}

Fix $S$ and refine further. Deleting (rather than contracting) the rungs of
a tree $T'$ with support $S$ leaves horizontal forests in the two levels;
projecting both to $G$ gives an edge multiset $U$ with multiplicities in
$\{1,2\}$: write $D$ for the doubled edges (used in both levels), $M$ for
the singletons ($m = |M|$, each carrying a free bit: which level), and
$N(S,U)$ for the number of trees with data $(S,U)$. Call a pair $(T,F)$ a
\emph{decomposition} of $(S,U)$ if $T$ is a spanning tree of $G$, $F$ is an
$S$-rooted spanning forest of $G$, and $T \uplus F = U$ as multisets; let
$d(S,U)$ be their number. Note that a decomposition determines its class:
$D = T\cap F$ and $M = T \,\triangle\, F$.

\begin{theorem}[class-level law]\label{thm:classlaw}
For every connected $G$, every nonempty $S \subseteq V(G)$, and every
multiset $U$,
\[
  N(S,U) \;=\; d(S,U)\cdot 2^{\,|S|-1}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Introduce a variable $w_e$ for each edge $e$ of $G$ and $z_u$ for each
$u \in V(G)$; in $G\times K_2$ weight \emph{both} copies of $e$ by $w_e$
and the rung at $u$ by $z_u$. As in Theorem~\ref{thm:support}, the weighted
Laplacian block-diagonalizes as $L_w \oplus (L_w + 2Z)$, where $L_w$ is the
weighted Laplacian of $(G,w)$ and $Z = \operatorname{diag}(z_u)$, and the
weighted tree sum of the prism,
$t(w,z) = \sum_{T'} \prod_e w_e^{\#\text{copies of $e$ in } T'}
\prod_{\text{rungs } u \in T'} z_u$, equals
$\tfrac12\,\tau(w)\,\det(L_w + 2Z)$ with $\tau(w)$ the weighted
spanning-tree sum of $G$. Expanding the determinant by principal minors in
$2Z$ and applying the weighted all-minors Matrix-Tree theorem,
\[
  t(w,z) \;=\; \tfrac12\,\tau(w) \sum_{R \subseteq V} 2^{|R|}
  \varphi_R(w) \prod_{u\in R} z_u,
\]
where $\varphi_R(w)$ is the weighted $R$-rooted spanning-forest sum. Both
sides are multilinear in the $z_u$; the coefficient of
$\prod_{u\in S} z_u$ gives
\[
  \sum_{T' :\, S(T') = S} \prod_e w_e^{\#\text{copies}}
  \;=\; 2^{|S|-1}\,\tau(w)\,\varphi_S(w).
\]
Now extract the coefficient of $w^U := \prod_{e \in D} w_e^2
\prod_{e\in M} w_e$. On the left this is $N(S,U)$. On the right, $\tau$ and
$\varphi_S$ are multilinear in the $w_e$ (a tree or forest uses each edge
at most once), so the coefficient of $w^U$ in the product counts exactly
the pairs $(T, F)$ with $T \uplus F = U$, i.e.\ $d(S,U)$.
\end{proof}

Setting $w \equiv 1$ recovers Theorem~\ref{thm:support}, so the class-level
law is the finest member of the family. It was found the other way around:
exhaustive computation on $Q_3$ and $Q_4$ (all $132 + 2\,459\,160$ classes,
from all $42\,467\,328$ spanning trees of $Q_4$) revealed the pattern
$N = d\cdot 2^{m-2s}$ with $s$ equal to the cycle rank of the support of
$U$, and the identity $m - 2\,\mathrm{rank} = |S|-1$ (an exercise with the
edge budgets) collapsed it to the statement above, after which the proof
was forced.

\begin{proposition}[polarization]\label{prop:polar}
Decompositions are themselves lifts: if $(T,F)$ decomposes $(S,U)$, then
placing $T\cap M$ on level $0$ and $F \cap M$ on level $1$ (with $D$ on
both) yields a spanning tree of $G\times K_2$ with data $(S,U)$. Hence
$d(S,U)$ counts the \emph{polarized} trees in their class --- those whose
level-$0$ graph is a spanning tree of $G$ and whose level-$1$ graph is an
$S$-rooted forest.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Level $0$ carries $D \cup (T\cap M) = T$, connected and acyclic; level $1$
carries $D \cup (F \cap M) = F$, a forest with $|S|$ components, one per
vertex of $S$. Each rung $u \in S$ joins the single level-$0$ component to
the level-$1$ component containing $u$, and those components are pairwise
distinct, so the component graph is a star --- a tree --- and the lift is a
spanning tree. Conversely a tree whose level-$0$ graph is spanning
connected and whose level-$1$ graph is $S$-rooted reads off a
decomposition.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[explicit bijection when the support is a tree]
\label{prop:rzero}
If the support of $U$ is a spanning tree of $G$ (equivalently $m = |S|-1$),
then $d \le 1$; when $d = 1$, \emph{every} one of the $2^m$ level
assignments yields a spanning tree, and the identity map on the $m = |S|-1$
free bits, with the polarized lift as base point, realizes
Theorem~\ref{thm:classlaw} bijectively.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
When $\operatorname{supp} U$ is a spanning tree, $T$ is forced to be that
tree and $F = D$, so $d = 1$ iff $D$ is $S$-rooted. For validity of every
assignment, induct: contract a leaf edge of the support lying in $D$
(harmless on both sides), and otherwise a leaf edge $e = (u,\ell) \in M$
has its leaf $\ell$ isolated in $D$, hence $\ell \in S$; whichever level
$e$ is assigned, the fiber of $\ell$ attaches to the rest by $e$ and the
rung at $\ell$, and deleting the fiber reduces to the same situation with
$(S\setminus \ell,\ M \setminus e)$. Both choices of the bit extend every
valid smaller assignment, giving the doubling and the explicit bijection.
\end{proof}

\section{The rooted normal form}\label{sec:rooted}

The missing map materializes in the \emph{rooted} setting, which the spin
experiments of Section~4 already singled out. For a spanning tree $L$ of
$G\times K_2$ with a root vertex $r$ and a rung $u \in S(L)$, define the
\emph{spin} $\sigma(u)\in\{0,1\}$ as the level of the endpoint of the rung
nearer to $r$ in $L$.

\begin{theorem}[all-spins-zero normal form]\label{thm:allzero}
A rooted tree $(L,r)$ of class $(S,U)$ has $\sigma \equiv 0$ if and only if
$L$ is polarized (Proposition~\ref{prop:polar}) and $r$ lies at level $0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The component graph $B$ of $L$ is a tree, \emph{bipartite between levels}
(every rung joins a level-$0$ component to a level-$1$ component). Root $B$
at the component of $r$; then $\sigma(u)$ is the level of the parent-side
component of the edge $u$. If $\sigma \equiv 0$, every parent is a level-$0$
component; by bipartiteness a level-$0$ component cannot have a level-$0$
parent, so no level-$0$ component has a parent at all: the level-$0$ side is
the single root component, whence $H_0$ is a spanning tree, and every
level-$1$ component is a leaf with exactly one rung, i.e.\ exactly one
vertex of $S$: $H_1$ is $S$-rooted, and $L$ is polarized with $r$ at level
$0$. The converse is immediate.
\end{proof}

Counting check: the polarized trees of a class number $d(S,U)$, each with
$|V(G)|$ level-$0$ roots, and $\sigma\equiv 0$ is one of $2^{|S|}$ spin
patterns among $2|V(G)|\cdot N(S,U)$ rooted trees; by
Theorem~\ref{thm:classlaw} the two counts agree, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:allzero} is consistent with --- and, given spin-class
equinumerosity, equivalent to --- the class-level law.

Spin-class equinumerosity within each class is exactly Bernardi's
independence theorem \cite[Thm.~1]{bernardi}, whose inductive proof is
constructive: the atomic move toggles the level of the single attaching
edge of a pendant fiber (his case of a vertex with no incoming projected
arc), and the general spin flip is a composition of such moves organized by
his induction. Composing that normalization with
Theorem~\ref{thm:allzero} and Proposition~\ref{prop:polar} yields:

\begin{theorem}[rooted straightening]\label{thm:rooted}
For every connected $G$ there is a bijection, explicit modulo the inductive
unwinding of \cite[Thm.~1]{bernardi},
\[
  \{(L, r) : L \text{ spanning tree of } G\times K_2,\ r \text{ a root}\}
  \;\longleftrightarrow\;
  \{(T, v)\} \times \{(F, \sigma)\},
\]
where $(T,v)$ is a rooted spanning tree of $G$ and $(F,\sigma)$ an
$S$-rooted spanning forest with a $\{0,1\}$-color on each root, summed over
$S$: i.e.\ the set form
$2|V|\, c(G\times K_2) = |V|\,c(G) \cdot \sum_S 2^{|S|} F_G(S)$ holds by
explicit correspondence, class by class.
\end{theorem}

We verified the constituent claims exhaustively over all $108\,384$ rooted
spanning trees of the prisms over nine base graphs (paths, cycles, cliques,
near-cliques; \texttt{qpark/scripts/rooted\_spins.py}).

\section{The hypercube Pr\"ufer code}\label{sec:prufer}

The pieces assemble. Write $\mathcal{F}_z(G)$ for the set of $z$-colored
rooted spanning forests of $G$ (each component carries a root with a color
from $[z]$), so $|\mathcal{F}_z(G)| = \det(zI + L_G)$.

\begin{theorem}[colored hierarchy, constructive]\label{thm:colored}
For every connected $G$ and $z \ge 1$ there is an explicit bijection
\[
  \mathcal{F}_z(G\times K_2) \;\longleftrightarrow\;
  \mathcal{F}_z(G) \times \mathcal{F}_{z+2}(G),
\]
obtained by composing (i) spin normalization --- for each rung of a forest,
the spin is the level of its endpoint nearer the root of its component, and
Bernardi's induction \cite[proof of Thm.~1]{bernardi}, specialized to the
Cartesian product, furnishes an explicit involution flipping any one spin
and nothing else --- with (ii) the colored star normal form: in an
all-spins-zero forest every rung-containing component collapses, by the
bipartite argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:allzero} applied per component, to a
level-$0$ rooted piece with level-$1$ leaves; level $0$ then reads off as an
element of $\mathcal{F}_z(G)$ and the level-$1$ pieces as an element of
$\mathcal{F}_{z+2}(G)$, a piece being either own-rooted (old color $< z$)
or rung-attached (root at its attachment vertex, color $z$ plus its
recorded spin --- the two new colors are the two spin values).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Pr\"ufer code for the hypercube]\label{thm:prufer}
Iterating Theorem~\ref{thm:colored} over the $n$ directions of $Q_n$
(the tree factor threading through with $z=1$ and its forest factor
entering the hierarchy at $z=2$) yields an explicit bijection
\[
  \{(T, r) : T \text{ spanning tree of } Q_n,\ r \text{ a root}\}
  \;\longleftrightarrow\;
  \prod_{k=1}^{n} \,[2k]^{\binom nk},
\]
i.e.\ every rooted spanning tree of the hypercube is encoded by, and
recovered from, one tuple of colors with $\binom nk$ coordinates of size
$2k$. In particular $2^n c(Q_n) = \prod_k (2k)^{\binom nk}$, which is
Stanley's formula \eqref{eq:stanley}, proved bijectively.
\end{theorem}

The bijection is algorithmic: the spin flip is defined by a terminating
recursion (delete-and-restore of pendant fibers and spliced degree-one
fibers, following \cite{bernardi}), and every other step is a closed-form
local rule. An implementation accompanies this repository
(\texttt{qpark/scripts/bijection.py}); it has been machine-verified to be a
bijection: exhaustively at the per-level statement for six $(G, z)$
combinations (up to $9\,800$ forests each, image confirmed equal to the
full product), exhaustively for $n = 3$ (all $3\,072$ rooted spanning trees
of $Q_3$ mapped onto the exact box $[2]^3\times[4]^3\times[6]$ with perfect
round-trips), and on samples for $n = 4$ ($200$ uniform rooted trees,
distinct codes in the box $[2]^4[4]^6[6]^4[8]$ of size $679\,477\,248$,
perfect round-trips). Whether a bijection whose one recursive ingredient is
unwound rather than closed-form meets every reader's standard for a
``direct'' bijective proof is a judgment we leave to the community; making
the spin normalization closed-form (Problem~\ref{prob:main}) remains the
natural refinement.

\section{Open problems}

All counting in the program is now proven, down to the finest stratum. The
sole remaining gap between the results above and a fully bijective proof of
\eqref{eq:stanley} is a \emph{map}:

\begin{problem}\label{prob:main}
Make the spin normalization self-contained and non-recursive for Cartesian
prisms: an explicit closed-form bijection between the spin classes of each
$(S,U)$, e.g.\ by exhibiting the composite of pendant flips directly. This
would make Theorem~\ref{thm:rooted} fully self-contained.
\end{problem}

\begin{remark}
An earlier draft posed the colored hierarchy as an open problem; it is now
Theorem~\ref{thm:colored}. The remaining refinement is
Problem~\ref{prob:main} alone.
\end{remark}
